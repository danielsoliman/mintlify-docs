Now, since I first dropped this thesis, I’ve seen a lot of people pushing back. I want to address the "low-signal" objections before we move forward.

**Objection 1: "Isn't this just 'Herd Instinct' that we evolved for survival?"**

I’m not saying we don't have instincts. Of course we do. We have the instinct to protect our kids, the instinct for food, and the instinct to help our tribe. That’s "Herd Instinct." But feeling a _desire_ to help is different from feeling like you _ought_ to help regardless of how you feel.

Imagine you hear a guy screaming for help in a burning building. You’re going to feel two conflicting impulses:

1. The desire to help (Herd Instinct).
2. The desire to run away and stay safe (Self-Preservation).

But you’ll find a third thing inside you—a "Judge"—that tells you that you _should_ follow the impulse to help and suppress the impulse to run. That thing that judges between two instincts, that decides which should be encouraged, cannot itself be either of them. It’s the "Sheet Music" that tells you which keys to press on the piano. The instincts are the keys; the Moral Law is the tune.

If the Moral Law was just an instinct, the stronger instinct would always win. But it’s usually the opposite. The Moral Law often tells us to side with the _weaker_ impulse. You want to be safe much more than you want to help the man who is drowning: but the Law tells you to help him anyway. The thing that tells you to "wake up" your sleeping instinct can't be the instinct itself.

Also, if the Moral Law was an instinct, we’d have one impulse that is _always_ "good." But we don't. Any instinct can be bad if followed at the wrong time. Even "motherly love" or "patriotism" can become toxic if they lead to injustice toward other people’s kids or other countries. There are no "good" or "bad" keys on a piano—every note is right at one time and wrong at another. The Moral Law is the "Master Composer" directing the instincts.

In fact, the most dangerous thing you can do is take one single impulse and make it your "Absolute Guide." If you follow "Love for Humanity" but ignore "Justice," you’ll end up lying and hurting individuals "for the greater good." You become a monster.

**Objection 2: "Isn't this just a Social Convention? Something we were taught?"**

This is a classic misunderstanding. Just because you learned something from your parents doesn't mean it’s a human invention. You learned the multiplication table in school. If you were stranded on an island, you wouldn't know it. Does that mean 2\+2=4 is just a "social convention" we made up? Of course not.

Some things we learn are conventions—like driving on the right side of the road or using certain slang. We could have picked the other side of the road, and the system would still work. But other things, like math, are objective truths. The Moral Law is like math.

Here’s why: First, while cultures vary slightly, the core "Protocol" is remarkably consistent across history. If you compare the ethics of the Egyptians, the Greeks, the Hindus, and the Chinese, the similarities are staggering.

Second, if morality were just a convention, there would be no such thing as "Moral Progress." Progress doesn't just mean "change"—it means "moving toward a better version." If no set of ideas is "truer" than another, there’s no reason to prefer modern human rights over the Nazis. But we all know some systems are better than others.

The moment you say one morality is "better" than another, you’re measuring them against a "Real Standard." You’re admitting there’s a "Real Morality" out there that exists independently of what we think. It’s like New York—it’s a real place. My map of New York might be better or worse than yours, but that’s only because the actual city exists. If "New York" was just a city we were each imagining in our heads, no one’s map could be "truer."

**Objection 3: The "Witch" Factor**

I’ve had people tell me, “Wait, 300 years ago they were executing witches. Was that 'Moral Progress'?”

Here’s the thing: we stopped executing witches because we stopped believing that witches exist. If we actually thought there were people who had "sold their souls" to a malicious power to kill their neighbors and cause mass destruction, we’d probably still have the death penalty for them today. That isn't a difference in _moral principle_; it’s a difference in _fact_. It’s an upgrade in our data, not a change in the underlying Human Protocol. You wouldn't call a guy "humane" for stopping his use of mousetraps if the only reason he stopped was that he realized he didn't have any mice.

The bottom line is this:

1. Humans everywhere have this idea that they _ought_ to behave a certain way. They can’t shake it.
2. Humans everywhere fail to actually behave that way.

We know the Protocol, and we break it. These are the two foundational facts if you want to understand yourself and the world we live in.
