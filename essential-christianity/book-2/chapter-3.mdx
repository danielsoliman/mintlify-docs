Okay, so here's one of the biggest objections people throw at Christianity: If God is all-powerful, why does evil exist? And if evil exists, either God allows it—which makes Him weird—or He can't stop it—which means He's not actually all-powerful.

It's a legitimate question. But here's what I've learned from actually running organizations and dealing with people: you can want something in one way and not want it in another way simultaneously. Sounds contradictory, but it's not.

## **The Free Will Paradox**

Think about a mother with kids. She's tired of nagging them to clean their room every single night. So she decides, "Alright, I'm stepping back. You're old enough to manage this yourself. Figure it out."

Then one night she goes upstairs and finds stuffed animals, ink bottles, textbooks—everything just scattered across the floor like a tornado hit.

Is this what she wants? No. She'd much prefer they keep it clean. But here's the thing: **her decision to let them be free created the possibility of this mess.** You can't have genuine freedom and control every outcome. Those things are contradictory.

Same principle applies in military units, corporations, trade unions, schools—anywhere you give people autonomy. You set something up as voluntary, and then half the people don't do it. That's not what you wanted, but your own choice to let them be free is what made it possible.

God's dealing with the same constraint.

## **Why God Created Free Creatures**

God made beings with free will. Real free will. That means we can genuinely choose right or wrong. Some people imagine a creature that's free but _can't_ go wrong. I don't think that's coherent. Freedom isn't freedom if you literally can't choose the bad option. If you're free to do good, you're free to do bad. That's what freedom means.

Now, why would God risk that? Why not just create robots that always do the right thing?

Because **free will is the only thing that makes love, goodness, and joy actually mean something.**

A world of automatons? Creatures that operate like machines? That's not worth creating. The happiness God designed for us—this profound spiritual union and ecstasy with Him and with each other—requires that we _choose_ it freely. That we _want_ to be united with Him. Not because we're programmed to, but because we genuinely love Him.

A world where everyone's forced to be good isn't better. It's not even a world anymore. It's a toy that God plays with by pulling strings.

God knew what would happen if we abused our freedom. He clearly thought the risk was worth it. Do we disagree? Well, here's the awkward part: **God is the source of your ability to reason in the first place.** You can't be right while He's wrong any more than a river can flow higher than its source. When you're arguing against God, you're using the very mental faculties God gave you to make that argument. It's like trying to saw off the branch you're standing on.

If God thinks this state of conflict in the universe is worth the price of having genuinely free creatures who can create real meaning and real impact—rather than a scripted world—then yeah, it's probably worth it.

### **Why Smart Creatures Are More Dangerous**

Here's a subtle point: **The better a creature is made, the more spectacularly it can fail.**

A cow isn't particularly good or bad. A dog has more capacity for both. A child can be better and worse still. An ordinary human being even more so. A genius? Way more potential for good or catastrophic harm. A superhuman spirit—best or worst of all.

That's why the most powerful beings pose the greatest risk. You're giving them more rope.

## **How Evil Started**

Now, how did the Dark Power—call him Satan—actually go wrong? We can't know for sure. But if we're using our own experience as a guide, here's what probably happened:

The moment you have a self—the moment you become aware that you exist as a separate being—there's immediately a temptation: _put yourself first._ Be the center. Want to be God, basically.

That was Satan's original sin. And that's what he sold to humanity.

Some people think the Fall of Man had something to do with sex. Nope. Wrong. The Genesis account actually suggests that sexual corruption _followed_ the Fall—it was a result, not the cause.

What Satan did was put an idea in our ancestors' heads: "You could be like gods. You could be independent. You could create your own happiness without needing God. You could be self-made."

And from that one broken idea came basically all of human history: money obsessions, poverty, rampant ambition, wars, prostitution, class systems, empires, slavery. The entire tragic story of humanity has been people desperately searching for _something other than God_ that will make them happy.

### **The Fatal Design Flaw**

Here's why it can never work:

God designed us. He engineered us like a manufacturer designs a machine. A car is built to run on gasoline. You could pour other liquids into it, but it won't run right.

> **God designed the human spirit to run on Him. He is the fuel we were built for. He's the only fuel.**

There is no alternative. That's not an opinion. That's just how it works.

So asking God to make us happy without Him is like asking a manufacturer to make a car run on water when it's engineered for gasoline. It's not that He won't. It's that _it's not possible._ There's no such thing as happiness and peace apart from God. It doesn't exist.

## **Why Every System Collapses**

This is the key to understanding history.

Civilizations build up massive amounts of energy. They create excellent institutions and systems. But then something always goes wrong. Some critical flaw emerges—and inevitably, the selfish and cruel people rise to the top. Then the whole thing slides back into misery and ruin.

The machine breaks down.

It looks good at first. Runs for a bit. Then it conks out.

Why? **They're running it on the wrong fuel.**

Humans keep trying to build meaning, happiness, and stability on power, money, status, ideology—anything except what we were actually built for. It's like pouring diesel into a gasoline engine and wondering why the whole thing seized up. Of course it fails. You're using the wrong fuel.

That's what Satan did to us. Convinced us we could run on something other than God.

## **God's Three-Part Response**

So what did God do about this mess?

**First:** He left us conscience. That inner sense of right and wrong. Throughout history, people have tried (some of them really hard) to follow it. But nobody ever quite succeeded. Not completely.

**Second:** He sent what I call "good dreams" throughout various religions—these weird recurring stories about a god who dies and comes back to life, and through his death gives new life to people. Something in humanity kept expecting this pattern. The narrative was embedded in our collective consciousness.

**Third:** He selected one specific people—the Jews—and spent centuries drilling into their heads what God actually is. One God. Not many. Not a pantheistic force. A being outside the universe who created it, radically different from anything else. He hammered this into their understanding through the entire Old Testament.

### **Then Something Shocking Happened**

And then, completely unexpectedly, a Jewish man shows up.

And this man starts talking like _He's God._

He forgives sins. All sins. He claims He's always existed. He says He's going to judge the world at the end of time. He talks and acts like He's the God of the universe.

Now, let's be clear about why this was so insane. In pantheistic religions, like Hinduism, someone could claim to be part of God or one with God—that wouldn't be that weird. But this is a _Jewish_ man. In Jewish theology, God meant something very specific: the One Being who exists outside the world, who created it, who is infinitely different from anything else.

What He was claiming was, without exaggeration, **the most shocking thing anyone has ever said.**

### **The Forgiveness Problem Nobody Notices**

Here's a part that's so familiar we miss how completely absurd it would be if it weren't true:

Jesus forgives people for sins.

Think about that. Unless you're God, this is absolutely insane.

I can forgive you for stepping on my toe. I can forgive you for stealing my money. Those offenses were against _me_. I'm the injured party. But imagine a random guy walking up to you and saying, "I forgive you for stepping on other people's toes. I forgive you for stealing from other people. I forgive you for hurting everyone you've wronged."

What kind of megalomaniac would do that? What kind of delusional arrogance is required to forgive sins you weren't the victim of?

Yet Jesus did exactly that. He forgave people without consulting anyone else they'd harmed. He acted as if _He_ was the primary person offended. He behaved as if He was the one whose laws were broken and whose love was wounded in every single sin.

This only makes sense if He actually _was_ God. If He really was the source of morality and the one violated by sin itself.

In anyone else's mouth, this would be the most staggering arrogance in human history. It would be delusional psychopathy.

## **The Disturbing Implication**

But here's what's strange: when His enemies read about what He said and did, they don't usually think, "This guy's a delusional narcissist." Neither do neutral observers. He tells us He's "humble and meek," and somehow we _believe_ Him—even though if He were just a human being, humility and meekness are literally the last words we could use to describe His behavior.

### **The Choice You Can't Avoid**

This is what I'm trying to stop people from saying: "I think Jesus was a great moral teacher, but I don't accept that He's God."

You can't say that. That's the one option that's actually _unavailable_ to you.

Here's why: A man who was only human and said the things Jesus said wouldn't be a great moral teacher. He'd be one of two things:

1. **A lunatic.** Like someone who genuinely believes they're a poached egg. Mental illness of that magnitude.

- **Something far worse.** A demon. A deliberately deceptive force pretending to be God.

There is no middle ground. You have to choose:

Either this man _was_ the Son of God, or He was insane/evil—possibly both.

You can dismiss Him as a fool. You can hate Him as a demon and kill Him for it. You can fall at His feet and worship Him as God.

**But you cannot have this patronizing middle position of "great teacher."** He didn't leave that option open. He didn't intend to.

That's not a compromise position. That's intellectual cowardice disguised as tolerance. The claims He made don't permit that escape route.
