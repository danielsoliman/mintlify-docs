**The Upgrade Path: Why the Best ROI in History Requires Skin in the Game**

Look, I need you to understand something fundamental about how transformation actually works.

Christ's move was the ultimate arbitrage. Perfect humility and surrender—not because he was weak, but because he had the full stack. He was running God-tier code but executing it in human hardware. That's the paradox that makes the whole thing work.

Here's what Christianity actually claims: if you can somehow get exposure to Christ's playbook—the suffering, the humility, the whole death-to-life protocol—you don't just get some inspirational quotes. You get the actual upgrade. You die to the old operating system and boot up something entirely new. We're talking about becoming the final, optimized version of what a human can be. Not self-help. Not "10% better." A completely different build.

This is way beyond just copying his strategies or reading his teachings.

People always ask: "When's the next evolution? When do humans level up?" But on the Christian thesis, **it already happened.** In Christ, a new species emerged. And the mechanism to spread that new life—the Christ-life protocol—is designed to be installed in us.

## **So How Does the Install Actually Work?**

Here's the thing: remember how you got your original life? You didn't opt in. You didn't fill out a form. Your parents passed it to you through a biological process that—let's be honest—if you described it to someone who'd never heard of it, they'd think you were insane. Pleasure, pain, risk, a nine-month deployment cycle. Extremely odd when you think about it.

The same God who architected _that_ system is the one who built the distribution mechanism for the Christ-life. And he didn't ask for our input on sex, and he's not asking for our input on this either. Expect it to be weird.

### **The Three Core Channels**

There are three primary ways the Christ-life gets transmitted:

1. **Baptism**
2. **Belief**
3. **That mysterious action** some call Holy Communion, some call the Mass, some call the Lord's Supper

These are the standard rails. I'm not saying there aren't edge cases or exceptions—maybe there are special circumstances where it works differently. But I don't have time to map every possible route, and frankly, I don't know enough. If someone asks you how to get to Edinburgh, you tell them about the trains. Yeah, they _could_ take a boat or a plane, but that's not the point.

And I'm not here to argue about which of these three is "most essential." My Methodist friends would want me to emphasize belief more. Others would weight the sacraments differently. I'm not getting into that fight. What I _will_ tell you is that anyone actually teaching Christian doctrine will tell you to use all three. That's enough signal for now.

### **Why These Specific Mechanisms?**

Honestly? I can't see _why_ these particular things should be the conductors of the new life. But then again, if you'd asked me before I knew how biology worked, I never would have guessed that a specific physical pleasure would result in a new human showing up nine months later.

**You have to take reality as it comes.** There's no ROI in complaining about what you _wish_ the system was or what you _expected_ it to be.

But even though I can't see _why_ it works this way, I can tell you _why I believe it's true._

I've already explained why I believe Jesus was—and is—God. And as a matter of history, it's pretty clear he taught his followers that this is how the new life spreads. In other words, **I believe it on his authority.**

Don't get scared by that word. "Authority" just means believing something because someone you trust told you. 99% of what you believe is on authority. You believe New York exists, right? You've probably never personally verified it. You believe in atoms, the solar system, evolution, how your blood circulates—all on authority, because scientists you trust say so. Every historical fact you know? Authority. None of us saw the Norman Conquest. None of us can prove it happened with pure logic like a math proof. We believe it because people who _were_ there left records.

A person who rejected authority in everything the way some people reject it in religion would literally know nothing their entire life.

## **This Isn't a Replacement for Doing the Work**

Don't misunderstand me. I'm not saying baptism, belief, and Holy Communion are _substitutes_ for your own efforts to follow Christ's example.

Think about it this way: your natural life came from your parents. But that doesn't mean it just _stays_ if you do nothing. You can lose it through neglect. You can destroy it through suicide. You have to feed it, maintain it, protect it. But here's the key: **you're not creating it.** You're only sustaining a life you received from someone else.

Same deal with the Christ-life. A Christian can lose it. He has to make efforts to keep it. But even the best Christian who ever lived isn't running on his own power. He's nourishing and protecting a life he could never have generated by himself.

And that has **massive practical implications.**

As long as natural life is in your body, it works to repair that body. Cut yourself, and up to a point, it heals. A dead body doesn't do that. A living body isn't one that never gets hurt—it's one that can repair itself.

Same with a Christian. A Christian isn't someone who never screws up. A Christian is someone who can repent, get back up, and start over after every failure—**because the Christ-life inside him is constantly repairing him,** enabling him to repeat (to some degree) the voluntary death that Christ himself executed.

## **The Asymmetry That Changes Everything**

This is why the Christian is in a fundamentally different position than other people trying to be good.

Most people hope that by being good, they'll please God (if he exists) or at least earn approval from other good people. But the Christian framework flips this: **any good a Christian does comes from the Christ-life inside him.**

Christians don't think God will love us _because_ we're good. They think God will _make_ us good because he loves us. It's like a greenhouse roof doesn't attract the sun because it's shiny—it becomes shiny _because the sun shines on it._

### **This Is Not Just a Metaphor**

Let me be extremely clear: when Christians say the Christ-life is in them, they don't just mean something mental or moral. When they talk about being "in Christ" or Christ being "in them," this isn't poetic language for "thinking about Christ" or "copying his example."

They mean **Christ is actually operating through them.** The whole collective of Christians is the physical organism through which Christ acts. We're his hands, his muscles, the cells of his body.

And maybe that explains a few things.

It explains why this new life spreads not just through mental acts like belief, but through physical acts like baptism and Holy Communion. This isn't just propagating an idea. **It's more like evolution—a biological or super-biological fact.**

There's no upside in trying to be "more spiritual" than God. God never intended humans to be purely spiritual beings. That's why he uses material things like bread and wine to transmit the new life. We might think that's crude or unspiritual. **God doesn't. He invented eating. He likes matter. He invented it.**

## **The Fairness Question**

Here's something that used to bother me: Isn't it massively unfair that this new life is limited to people who've heard of Christ and can believe in him?

But here's the truth: **God hasn't told us what his arrangements are for other people.** We know no one can be saved except through Christ. We _don't_ know that only those who know him can be saved through him.

But in the meantime, if you're worried about people on the outside, the most irrational thing you can do is **stay outside yourself.**

Christians are Christ's body—the organism through which he works. Every person who joins that body enables him to do more. If you want to help people outside, you need to add your own cell to the body of Christ, who alone can actually help them. Cutting off someone's fingers would be a strange strategy for getting them to do more work.

## **Why Doesn't God Just Force the Issue?**

Another objection: Why is God operating in stealth mode in this enemy-occupied world, running what looks like a secret society to undermine the devil? Why not just invade in force? Is he not strong enough?

Christians believe **he is going to land in force.** We don't know when. But we can guess why he's delaying.

#### **He wants to give us the chance to join his side freely.**

Think about it: you and I wouldn't have much respect for someone who waited until the Allies were marching into Berlin and _then_ announced they were on our side.

God will invade. But I wonder if people who ask God to intervene openly in our world realize what it will actually be like when he does.

**When that happens, it's game over.** When the author walks onto the stage, the play ends.

God is going to invade, all right. But what's the point of saying you're on his side _then,_ when you see the entire natural universe dissolving like a simulation shutting down, and something else—something you never conceived of—comes crashing in? Something so beautiful to some and so terrible to others that none of us will have any choice left?

Because this time it will be **God without the veil.** Something so overwhelming it will trigger either irresistible love or irresistible horror in every person. It will be too late then to choose sides. There's no use saying you choose to lie down when it's become impossible to stand up.

That won't be the time for choosing. **That will be the time when we discover which side we actually chose,** whether we realized it or not.

**Now. Today. This moment.** This is our window to choose the right side.

God is holding back to give us that chance. It won't last forever.

We have to take it or leave it.
