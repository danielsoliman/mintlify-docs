"Fake it till you make it" has become one of the most mocked phrases in the startup world.

And for good reason. We've watched founders fake revenue, fake users, fake blood tests, and fake balance sheets. We've seen what happens when the performance replaces the product. Lawsuits. Collapses. Documentaries.

But I want to make a case today that sounds crazy at first: **there is a version of pretending that is the single most important thing you will ever do.** Not the fraud version. The other one. The one that turns into the real thing.

Up to now, this series has been about the facts. What God is. What He did. The architecture of the whole system. Today we shift gears. Today is about execution. What do you actually _do_ with all of this?

And the answer starts tonight.

---

## Two stories to load into your head

Look, before we get into the mechanics, I want to give you two mental models.

The first is the old fairy tale everyone knows. A woman ends up committed to a monster. She treats it as if it were a man. She shows it the kind of love you'd show a real person. And eventually, it actually becomes one.

The second is a story about a guy who has to wear a mask for years. The mask makes him look better than he actually is. Kinder. More handsome. More trustworthy. And when he finally takes it off, he discovers something strange: his real face has grown into the shape of the mask. The disguise became the reality.

Keep both in your back pocket. They're fanciful, but they point at something real.

---

## The most audacious two words you'll ever say

Here's the thing. If you've read this far into the series, you're probably curious enough to try praying. And if you pray anything at all, there's a decent chance you'll land on the Lord's Prayer.

It opens with two words: _Our Father._

Stop and actually process what you're doing when you say that. You are claiming the position of a son of God. You are stepping into the role that belongs to Christ. You are, to put it bluntly, **dressing up as Him.**

And the moment you see it clearly, you also see the problem. You are not that. Christ's will is perfectly aligned with the Father's. Yours isn't. You're a walking bundle of self-interest: anxieties, ambitions, envy, ego, cravings, all running on a biological clock with a hard expiration date.

It's a pre-seed founder introducing himself as CEO of a trillion-dollar company. Absurd nerve.

And yet here's the twist: **you were told to do it.** This isn't a presumption you came up with. It's the instruction.

---

## Two kinds of pretending

So why on earth would you be asked to pretend to be something you're not?

Here's the fascinating thing. Even at the purely human level, there are two completely different kinds of pretending, and people confuse them all the time.

**The bad kind is a substitute.** The pretense sits where the real thing should be. Your contractor tells you he's "on it" while doing nothing. A company announces a "commitment to sustainability" with zero budget behind it. The show replaces the substance. That's fraud. That's the version that earned "fake it till you make it" its bad reputation.

**The good kind is an on-ramp.** The pretense leads to the real thing. You walk into a meeting tired, irritated, not feeling generous toward anyone. But you know you should be. So you act friendly anyway. You ask the junior analyst how her weekend was. You listen. And fifteen minutes later? You actually _are_ friendlier. The behavior pulled the feeling along behind it.

This is one of the most underrated truths about human performance: **a lot of the time, behavior is the installer. You run the actions first, and the trait gets downloaded afterward.**

Watch kids. They play adult constantly: lemonade stands run like a Fortune 500, fake YouTube channels. And the whole time they're building real skills. The make-believe adulthood is exactly how they become adults for real.

That's the good kind. And that's the kind we're talking about.

---

## The pretense starts paying off immediately

Here's what's wild. The second you consciously think, _"I'm dressing up as Christ right now,"_ you'll almost certainly spot something you could change right now to make the costume a little less fake.

Maybe there's a resentful thought looping in your head that would never be running in the mind of an actual son of God. Okay. Kill it.

Maybe you realize you shouldn't be sitting here praying at all. You should be answering the text from your friend who's been going through a hard month. Or doing the dishes so your partner doesn't have to. Okay. Go do it.

See what just happened? You weren't just playing a role. The role started making demands. And the person making those demands isn't you.

---

## This is not just your conscience talking

Now, the skeptic says: "Pomp, that's just a dramatic way of describing your conscience."

No. And the distinction matters.

If you ask your conscience a question, you get one answer. If you ask "what would this look like if I were actually trying to be like Christ," you get a very different one.

Your conscience is basically a compliance department. It flags the obvious violations. It tells you not to lie, not to steal, not to cheat. But there's a massive gray zone it doesn't touch, especially inside your own head. The petty grudge. The smug comparison. The quiet contempt for someone who annoyed you. Compliance won't flag any of that. But the moment you're trying to resemble a specific Person, it all becomes obviously incompatible.

**You've stopped running a rules engine. You're trying to catch something from someone.** Lewis calls it "the good infection."

Think about the difference between following an employee handbook and being mentored by the best operator you've ever met. The handbook gives you rules. The mentor gives you _a way of being_ that you absorb by proximity. One is checklist compliance. The other is more like painting a portrait: you keep looking at the subject and adjusting.

And here's the paradox. In one sense it's way harder than keeping the rules, because the bar is a whole person, not a list. In another sense it's way easier, because you're not doing it alone. The subject of the portrait is in the room.

---

## The tin soldier problem

Here's the core claim, and I want to state it plainly.

Christ isn't a historical reference point. He's present, standing beside you, and He's running a conversion process on you. The goal is to remake you in His own category. He's pushing His kind of life, the Zoe we talked about at the beginning of this book, into a system that has only ever run on biological life.

Picture a tin toy soldier mid-conversion into a living human being. Some of it is already warm. Some of it is still metal. **Whatever inside you pushes back on the process? That's the metal talking.**

That one line reframes every internal objection you'll ever feel. The friction isn't evidence that something is wrong. It's evidence of what hasn't been converted yet.

---

## "But I've never felt that"

Look, some of you are reading this and thinking: "I've never once felt helped by some invisible Christ. But I've been helped plenty by real people. My mom. A coach. A friend who showed up when things fell apart."

Here's my answer. That's like someone during a supply chain crisis saying, "The wheat shortage won't affect me. I only eat bread." The bread _is_ the wheat. If there's no wheat, there's no bread.

Every good thing a human ever did for you was sourced somewhere. Shut down the supplier and the retailers have nothing on the shelves. Christ is upstream. The people in your life are the distribution layer.

And the distribution network is huge. He works through nature. Through your body. Through books, podcasts, conversations at 2am. Sometimes through experiences that look, at the time, like the opposite of faith. Here's a counterintuitive one: a college kid who's been showing up to church out of habit finally admits to himself that he doesn't buy it, and walks away. If that exit is driven by real honesty, and not a move to get a rise out of Mom and Dad, he may be standing nearer to Christ in that moment than at any point in his years of autopilot attendance.

But above everything else, He works through **other people.**

---

## People are the network

Here's where my network-effects brain lights up.

Humans are carriers. Nodes. We pass this thing to each other. Sometimes without even knowing we're doing it. People who don't have it themselves can still transmit it. Lewis himself was moved toward Christianity by people who weren't Christians. But usually it travels from those who know Him to those who don't.

That's why the Church matters. Not the building. Not the institution as a brand. The network of people reflecting Him back to each other.

And the math isn't linear. Metcalfe's Law says a network's value grows faster than its node count. Lewis was making the same point long before anyone was pitching social apps: put two people side by side chasing Christ and you don't get a 2x. You get something closer to **a 16x.** That's not addition. That's compounding.

---

## Don't confuse the node with the source

But here's the risk management piece, and do not skip it.

An infant gets fed long before it has any concept of who's feeding it. Nothing wrong with that. Early on, you're in the same spot: you notice the friend who showed up, not the One operating through the friend.

But you can't stay a newborn. You have to eventually recognize the actual source.

Why? Because if you don't, you've built your entire portfolio on a single counterparty, and that counterparty is human. Your heroes will blow calls. Your mentors will get things wrong. And there's a 100% mortality rate across the whole group.

Be grateful to the people who helped you. Honor them. Love them. **But never, ever put all your faith in one human being.** Not the smartest founder you know. Not the wisest pastor. Not the greatest mentor of your life. That's concentration risk at the deepest level of your existence.

Beach sand makes great sandcastles. Nobody sane pours a skyscraper foundation on it.

---

## This is not a reading program

Now the strange phrases in the New Testament start making sense. Being "born again." "Putting on" Christ. Christ being "formed in" you. Having "the mind of Christ."

Get this idea out of your head immediately: that those are just poetic ways of saying "read what Jesus said and try your best to apply it." That's how you'd approach Plato, or Marx, or a Warren Buffett shareholder letter. Study the material, implement the lessons.

That is not what's being described.

What's being described is a **live Person**, right now, in the room where you're praying, actively doing something to you. Not a great teacher who died two thousand years ago and left behind a solid reading list. Someone alive today, every bit as human as you, every bit as divine as on day one of creation, getting root access to your operating system. Deprecating the legacy self. Deploying His own build in its place.

The rollout is staged. A few seconds of the new build here and there. Then whole afternoons. And if the deployment completes, it becomes the permanent production version of you: a fundamentally new type of being, a scaled-down Christ, plugged into the same kind of life God runs on, with all the strength, delight, understanding, and timelessness that comes with it.

And once that process starts, you make two discoveries.

---

## Discovery one: the rats in the cellar

You start noticing that the problem isn't just your bad actions. It's your **character.** You get worried not only about what you did, but about what you are.

Here's how this shows up in real life. At the end of the day, you look back at where you fell short. Nine times out of ten, the worst moment was some failure of basic love. You snapped at someone. You were curt. Dismissive. Sarcastic. You fired off a DM you shouldn't have sent.

And your brain's instant defense is: "It came out of nowhere. I got blindsided. I didn't have time to think."

Fine. That's a mitigating factor. A premeditated outburst would be worse.

But think about it from a due-diligence perspective. **What someone does when they're caught off guard is the cleanest data you'll ever get on who they really are.** Investors know this. You don't learn about a founder from the pitch deck. You learn about them when the deal blows up at 11pm and they don't have time to perform.

Lewis has a great image for this, and I'll put it my way. Imagine a basement with a rodent problem. Stomp down the stairs announcing yourself and everything has vanished by the time you hit the switch. Kill the lights, walk in quietly, flip them on in one move, and now you see what's actually living down there. The quick flip didn't populate the basement. It just denied the rodents their head start.

Same with your temper. The surprise didn't install the irritability. It just ran a pop inspection before you could tidy up.

And here's the problem: **you don't have admin access to that basement.** Willpower can throttle your behavior, sometimes. It can't reach down and recode your disposition.

It's the same with your good actions, honestly. Pull the logs on your best moments and check the motive field. How much of it was image management? Worry about the group chat's opinion? Wanting credit? Or just a stubborn, I'm-better-than-this streak that could have powered something ugly just as easily if the setup had been different?

You can't give yourself new motives by trying harder. Motives aren't a setting you can toggle.

Follow the logic. Your actions are mostly lagging indicators of your character. Character is the asset that actually matters. And character is exactly the thing your effort can't directly move. So the most important upgrade on your roadmap is one you can't ship yourself. Get even a little way down this road and it becomes obvious: the deep work is God's department, not yours.

---

## Discovery two: you were never the one doing it

That leads to a correction. Everything in this chapter so far has sounded like _you're_ the active party. You pretend. You dress up. You do the work.

Flip that. **The real operator is God. Your role is basically to not block the deployment.**

Take it one step further: the biggest act of pretending in this whole story isn't yours. It's His.

He has full visibility into the data. Selfish. Grabby. Whiny. Defiant. No illusions. And He makes a deliberate call anyway: mark this asset at the valuation of a Son. There's already some basis for it, since Christ took on a human nature, so you share that much. The rest is a forward-looking mark. He prices you at what you'll be, not what you are, because that pricing is how the gap gets closed.

**The Father underwrites you at the valuation of His Son. The Son sits right beside you doing the work to hit that number.**

Strange idea? A cosmic game of make-believe? Maybe. But look at how anything gets elevated in practice. The stronger party almost always pulls the weaker one up by acting like the upgrade already happened.

Parents chat with their infants like they're following the conversation. Months of one-sided dialogue, and that's precisely the curriculum that teaches speech. People talk to their golden retrievers like roommates, and somehow the dogs end up uncannily tuned in to human life.

The best managers do this too. They treat a junior employee like the leader they could become, and the employee grows into the role. The best VCs back founders based on who they'll be in ten years, not who they are on demo day.

You get raised up by being treated as more than you are.

---

## The bottom line

Here's the realization I want you to walk away with.

"Fake it till you make it" fails when the faking is the whole strategy. When the pretense is a substitute for reality, it collapses. Every time.

But this is a different trade entirely. You put on the costume because you were told to. The costume immediately starts making real demands. And the Person whose costume you're wearing is standing next to you, actively converting the fake into the real, piece by piece, from tin into flesh.

You are not the operator here. You are the asset being transformed.

**Say "Our Father" tonight. Mean it as a pretense. And then get out of the way while it stops being one.**
