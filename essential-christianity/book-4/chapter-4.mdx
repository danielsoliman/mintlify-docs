Most people think the best things in life get handed out like bonuses.

You hit your numbers, the board approves the package, and the reward lands in your account. Clean. Transactional. Earned.

Today I'm going to argue that this model is wrong, at least for the things that matter most. Joy, peace, real power, life that doesn't run out. Those don't get wired to you. You catch them. And you only catch them by getting close to the source.

But to get there, we have to take a short detour through some of the strangest territory in the whole Christian framework. Stay with me. The payoff is worth it.

---

## Cause doesn't always come first

Look, start with a simple picture.

Put your phone on a wireless charging stand on your desk. The stand is holding the phone up. Without the stand, the phone lies flat on the desk. So the stand's position is the reason for the phone's position. The stand is the cause. The phone's angle is the effect.

Now do a thought experiment. It's impossible in real life, but go with it. Imagine that phone has been sitting on that stand forever. No beginning. No moment when somebody set it down.

In that world, the phone's position has always depended on the stand. But the stand was never there first. There was no "before." The cause and the effect have existed side by side for exactly the same amount of time.

Here's the thing: we're trained to assume causes come first. You order the late-night burrito, and the regret shows up at 2am. Input, then output. That's how most of our lives work.

**But not every cause sits upstream of its effect in time.** Some causes are only upstream in logic. Remember that, because everything that follows depends on it.

---

## The Father and the Son problem

In the last chapter we landed on the idea that God is one Being who contains three Persons. A cube contains six squares and is still one cube. Weird, but coherent.

The moment you try to explain how those Persons relate to each other, though, the language starts lying to you.

Christianity calls the First Person the Father and the Second Person the Son. It says the Father begets the Son. Not builds, not makes. Begets. Because what comes out is the same kind of thing as where it came from. We covered this in Chapter 1: you build an app, but you beget a kid.

So "Father" is the right word. But it has a bug. Every human father exists before his kid. You can't have a son born before you were. So the word sneaks in an assumption of sequence.

**That assumption is false.** The Son's existence depends entirely on the Father's. Yet you could rewind the timeline as far as you like and never find a version of history where the Father was running solo. It's the phone on the stand again: one thing leans on the other, and neither one got there first.

---

## The best analogy is running in your head right now

The fascinating thing here is that you just ran a live demo of this without noticing.

When I asked you to picture the phone on the stand, you did it. You made a decision to imagine, and an image showed up. Your act of imagining was the cause. The mental image was the effect.

But did you imagine first and then wait for the image to load? No. There was no buffering. The instant you chose to picture it, the picture was there. And for as long as you kept choosing to hold it, it stayed. The moment you stopped, it disappeared. The cause and the effect started together and ended together.

Now scale that up. Picture a mind that has always existed and has always been holding one particular thought. That act of thinking would always be producing its result. And the result would be exactly as old as the act. Eternal cause. Eternal effect. No gap.

That's about as close as analogy gets to the Christian description of the Son. Think of a broadcast that never had a start date. The signal pours out of the source continuously, the way light pours off a bulb or warmth radiates off a campfire or an idea surfaces from a mind. The Son is the Father's message, fully expressed. The transmission has been live since before there was a "since." There's no archive of dead air.

---

## Why the old language wins anyway

Okay, now notice what just happened.

I traded "Father and Son" for broadcasts, bulbs, campfires, and mental images. Useful. But every one of those analogies makes it sound like we're talking about two *things*. A bulb and its glow. A campfire and its warmth. Hardware and output.

Christianity isn't claiming two things. It's claiming two *Persons*.

So the upgrade actually turned out to be a downgrade. The original "Father and Son" language was more accurate than anything I swapped in.

This is a pattern every builder has lived through. You think you can improve on the core product. You rip out the old design, ship your clever new version, and then the users tell you the original was better. Sometimes the legacy system was legacy for a reason.

**It's fine to step away from the original wording to make one point clearer. But you always have to come back.** If anyone is going to know the best way to describe God, it's going to be God, not us.

And the most important data point buried in that Father-Son language is simple: **the relationship runs on love.** The Father takes deep joy in the Son. The Son honors the Father. Strip everything else away and that's the core.

---

## "God is love" doesn't compute with only one Person

Here's where this stops being abstract and starts having real implications.

Everybody likes quoting "God is love." It's on coffee mugs. It's in Instagram captions. People love the line.

But run the logic. Love needs a counterparty. It's a two-sided relationship by definition, like a trade that requires both a buyer and a seller. A market with one participant isn't a market.

So if God were just one solitary Person, then before anything else existed, there was nobody to love. Which means that before creation, God wasn't love. The line breaks.

**"God is love" only makes sense if God contains at least two Persons.** The Trinity isn't some theological add-on that complicates a simple idea. It's the thing that makes the simple idea actually work.

Honestly, most people quoting that line have flipped it without realizing. What they're really saying is closer to "love is God." In plain terms: any loving emotion a human feels deserves reverence, no matter its source or where it ends up. You can debate that. But it's a different thesis entirely.

Christianity's thesis is bigger. Love isn't a feeling God has. It's an action that has been in motion inside God with no start date, and the entire universe is downstream of it.

---

## God isn't a static asset

Look, this might be the biggest difference between Christianity and every other religious framework out there.

In this framework, God isn't a fixed object sitting on a shelf. God isn't even a lone individual. God is a process, an ongoing exchange, something with momentum. Closer to a live performance than a statue. Give me a little latitude and I'd call it choreography.

Now the wild part. The connection between the Father and the Son carries so much life that it doesn't stay a connection. It becomes someone. The relationship is itself a Person.

I know. That one breaks the brain. So let's ground it.

Think about the best founding teams you've ever seen. When the right people come together, something emerges that none of them had alone. Every great company has a culture. People talk about the "spirit" of the team. They develop shared language, inside jokes, a way of attacking problems, a rhythm. Pull any one person out and drop them somewhere else, and they don't act the same way. Put them back together, and it shows up again. It's almost like the group has its own personality.

Of course, a company culture isn't literally a person. It's only *like* one. That's one of the gaps between us and God.

**What emerges from the shared life of the Father and the Son isn't just like a person. It is a Person.** That's the Third Person of the Trinity. Christianity calls Him the Holy Spirit, the "spirit" of God.

---

## Why the Spirit feels blurry, and why that's fine

Here's the thing: if the Holy Spirit seems fuzzier in your head than the Father or the Son, you're not doing it wrong. That's expected, and there's a logic to it.

You usually aren't looking *at* the Spirit. The Spirit is working *through* you.

Map it spatially. The Father is the one you face. The Son is beside you, coaching you through prayer, working to make you into a son as well. The Spirit is the one operating from inside you or from behind you. He's the background process, not the app on the screen. Nobody stares at their operating system. You notice what it makes possible.

For some people, the smart move is to start with the Spirit and reverse-engineer the rest. Start with the claim that God is love. That love shows up working through actual people, and most of all through the Christian community as a whole. Then trace it upstream: that same love has been moving back and forth between the Father and the Son for all of eternity.

---

## So what? Everything.

Now the obvious question. Why should a person running a business, raising a family, and managing their portfolio care about any of this?

Because it matters more than anything else you'll read this year.

This three-Person pattern isn't just something to admire on a whiteboard. It's supposed to get installed in you. Flip it around and it's the same claim: you have to join the pattern yourself and find your spot in it. **That's the only path to the kind of happiness humans were actually designed for.**

And this is where the title comes in.

Good things spread the same way bad things do. By contact. By infection.

Want a tan? You have to go outside. Want to be soaked? You have to jump in the ocean. Nobody ever got sunburned reading a weather app.

> **If you want joy, peace, power, and life that doesn't expire, you have to get close to the thing that has them. Or better, get inside it.**

This is network effects at the deepest level. Value doesn't come from owning the asset in isolation. It comes from being connected to the network where the value actually lives.

And notice what this kills. These aren't rewards God keeps in a vault and airdrops into random wallets at His discretion. That's the bonus model again, and it doesn't fit. Picture something more like a geyser of raw energy and beauty erupting at the dead center of reality. Get near it and you get drenched. Keep your distance and you leave bone dry. Nobody's gatekeeping. That's just the physics of being near the source.

Follow that through. If you're connected to the source of life itself, how would you ever run out of it? And if you've disconnected from it, what's left except a slow fade?

---

## How you actually plug in

Fine. So how does a human being actually get pulled into that three-Person life?

Go back to the framework from Chapter 1. God didn't beget us. He built us. In our default state we aren't sons of God. We're more like statues. Beautiful, maybe. Impressive engineering. But not the same kind of thing as their maker.

We run on Bios, biological life. The startup on VC money. Real, growing, and on a clock. Eventually the runway ends.

What we don't have is Zoe, the self-sustaining, uncreated life that exists in God.

So here's the entire pitch Christianity makes, with the noise removed: **stop fighting God's plan for you, and you get access to the life Christ himself is living.**

And Christ's life isn't built. It's begotten. It has always existed and always will. Share in that life, and you become a son of God too. You start loving the Father the way the Son does. And the Holy Spirit starts rising up in you.

That's the reason Christ entered the world as a human in the first place. Not to drop a better set of life hacks. To transmit His kind of life to other people, one contact at a time. Lewis has a two-word label for it: "good infection."

---

## The bottom line

Here's what I want you to walk away with.

We spend most of our lives running the bonus model. Perform, get rewarded. Hit targets, collect the payout. And we assume the deepest goods in life work the same way: that if we stack up enough good behavior, someone will eventually wire us joy and peace and forever.

That's not how this system works.

**The best things aren't payouts. They're properties of a source. And the only way to get them is to get close enough to catch them.**

God isn't a static figure handing out prizes. God is a living, loving, three-Person dance that has been running forever. And the invitation isn't to admire it from a distance. It's to step into it.

The goal is for every Christian to become a small-scale copy of Christ. That's not a perk of the program. That's the entire program. Nothing else is on the roadmap.

_Proximity is the whole game. Everything else is noise._
