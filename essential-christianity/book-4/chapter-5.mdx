Every great product has an adoption problem.

You can build something ten times better than the incumbent, and the user will still fight you. Not because the new thing is bad. Because the old thing is _familiar_, and familiar feels safe, and safe feels like survival.

This week I want to talk about the biggest adoption problem in history. It's not about software. It's about you. And it explains why the most valuable upgrade ever offered to our species is also the one we resist the hardest.

Let's get into it.

---

## The thesis in one line

Here's the whole deal structure, compressed:

**God's Son took on our nature so that we could take on his.**

That's the trade. Everything else in this piece explains how it works and why we keep trying to walk away from the table.

In the last few chapters, we laid out the two operating systems. **Bios** is biological life: carbon-based, input-dependent, guaranteed to decay. **Zoe** is the life that exists in God: uncreated, self-sustaining, no expiration date. The entire Christian pitch is that Bios can be upgraded into Zoe.

Could the rollout have looked different? Maybe. In a world where humanity never defected to the other side, perhaps the upgrade would have shipped as a default setting, with every person onboarded at birth and zero friction. I have no idea, and neither does anyone else. I don't trade on hypotheticals. We have to deal with the market as it actually exists today.

---

## Here's the thing: the two systems aren't just different, they're at war

Look, Bios and Zoe were always going to be different. A photograph is not the mountain. That part was baked in from the start.

But in the world we actually live in, there's an extra problem. **The two aren't just distinct. They're hostile to each other.**

Run an honest audit on what your natural life wants. It wants to be the main character. It wants likes, followers, validation. It wants to extract value from every relationship and every system it touches. It wants the whole universe optimized around its preferences.

And more than anything, it wants zero oversight.

It avoids anything that outranks it. Anything that might expose how small it really is. It's the founder who refuses to bring on a world-class board because he can't stomach being told he's wrong. It's the guy who skips the elite gym because standing next to real athletes makes his own conditioning look embarrassing.

Here's the fascinating part: **the natural self has read the situation correctly.**

It understands the terms. Let the new life in, and the ego doesn't get a tune-up. It gets shut down. The self-will, the self-obsession, the constant me-first calculus — none of that survives the merger. So the natural self does what every incumbent does when it sees an existential threat coming. It goes to war.

Blockbuster didn't lose because it couldn't see Netflix coming. It lost because seeing it clearly meant admitting the old business had to die. Your natural self is Blockbuster. It would rather go down swinging than be replaced.

---

## The obstinate toy soldier

Every kid has had some version of this daydream: the action figures on the shelf wake up and start moving on their own.

Let's actually model it. Say you've got a little metal soldier, and you have the power to make him a living, breathing person. The only way to do it is to swap out the metal for living tissue. There's no shortcut.

Now suppose he refuses.

Living tissue means nothing to him. He has no reference point for breathing, feeling, thinking, or loving. What he _can_ see is his metal body being dismantled piece by piece. From where he's standing, this isn't an upgrade. **It's a murder in progress.** So he resists with every bit of metal he's got.

That's us.

We're staring down the most valuable transformation imaginable, and all we can see is what we're giving up. We can't price the upside because we've never experienced it. We can only price the downside, and the downside looks like death.

This is loss aversion at its most extreme. Behavioral economists have shown for decades that people feel losses roughly twice as hard as equivalent gains. Now push that to infinity. The gain is a mode of existence you literally cannot picture. The loss is _everything you currently think you are_. Of course the soldier digs in.

---

## The founder goes into the product

So what was God's play against an entire army of stubborn metal soldiers?

He didn't send a memo. He didn't push an update from the cloud and hope we'd click accept.

**He enlisted.**

The Son — the Second Person of God — showed up as a real human being. Not a vague spiritual vibe. A particular guy, from a particular town, with a particular face, who spoke one particular dialect and had to eat lunch like everybody else. The mind behind every galaxy started out as an infant. And before that, an embryo, growing in the dark like every one of us did.

Want to feel the scale of that? Picture yourself voluntarily downgrading into a garden slug, permanently. Now understand that the real gap is incomparably wider than that.

The best founders I know don't run their companies from a corner office. They go to the front lines. They answer support tickets. They use the product the way the customer does, bugs and all. This is founder-mode taken to the absolute limit: **the architect of the entire system logged in as an ordinary user, with no admin privileges held in reserve.**

---

## Proof of concept

Here's what that produced: for the first time, the spec was actually met.

In Jesus, the ordinary human nature he inherited from his mother put up zero resistance to the upgrade. It handed itself over completely, and the divine Son absorbed all of it. **One human being made the full transition.** The upgrade had shipped, at least once.

Now look at the life he chose. If the core problem is that the old self has to go through a kind of death, then he picked a path that put his own natural wants to death over and over. He was broke. His own relatives thought he'd lost it. One of his inner circle sold him out. The authorities mocked him, beat him, and then killed him in the most brutal way the empire had on the menu.

He didn't take the easy route and then tell us to take the hard one. He took the hardest route available and ran the process end to end.

And then the part nobody saw coming: after all that dying — the final one on the cross and the thousand small ones before it — he came back. And because his humanity had been fused with the divine Son, that humanity came back too.

This is the crucial part, so I'll highlight it:

> **The resurrection wasn't God escaping a human costume. A human being walked out of that tomb.**

That's the whole point. That's the proof of concept. For the first time, we got to see the finished product: what a person is supposed to look like. One soldier, built from the same metal as the rest of us, fully and gloriously alive.

## Where the analogy breaks: network effects

Now I have to be honest about the limits of the toy soldier comparison.

Toys on a shelf are independent units. If one of them wakes up, the others are completely unaffected. No shared state. No connection.

People are different. We only _seem_ independent because we experience life one moment at a time. Everyone walking around in their own body, scrolling their own phone.

Widen the time horizon and that illusion falls apart. You didn't come from nowhere. At one point you were physically inside your mom. Trace it back a step and you were genetic code split between two parents, and back another step, four grandparents. Lay out the entire human story the way God sees it — all of time at once — and you don't get eight billion isolated dots. **You get one living, branching organism.** Something like a massive family tree that's actually a single tree, every branch drawing from the same root system.

And it goes deeper. We aren't disconnected from God, either. Your next breath happens because he's actively sustaining you right now. You're not a standalone app. You're a process running on infrastructure you didn't build and can't see.

So when Christ joined the human race, it wasn't one isolated toy waking up. It was the force that already powers the whole network starting to operate on it in a brand-new way, beginning at one specific node. From there, the change ripples outward through the system.

And it doesn't only ripple forward. It reaches backward in history, too, and outward to people who will never hear the name Jesus.

Think about a protocol upgrade. When a core change ships to a network, it doesn't just affect the wallets that downloaded it on day one. It changes the rules of the whole chain, including how every prior block is read. Or think about a flavor concentrate: a few drops in a pitcher, and every glass you pour afterward tastes different.

Are these analogies perfect? Not even close. God has no true comparable. Nothing he does maps cleanly onto anything else, and frankly you should be suspicious of anyone who claims it does.

---

## The heavy lifting is already done

So what actually changed for the whole human network?

Here it is: **the impossible part of the transformation is already complete.**

Turning a manufactured thing into a family member — moving from the life that expires to the life that doesn't — was the one step no human could pull off alone. That step is done. As a species, we've been rescued on paper. Each of us still has to cash the check personally. But the work we could never have done is off the table.

This is the most important reframe in the whole piece. Most people treat spirituality like a summit attempt. You grind, you self-optimize, you stack habits, and maybe someday you reach the top on your own effort.

That's not the model. **You don't have to scale the mountain. The summit relocated to base camp.**

It's the difference between building your own payment rails from scratch and plugging into rails that already work. Make yourself available to the one person who carried this new life completely — fully divine, and also fully, genuinely human — and he runs the transformation for you, from the inside.

Remember the "good infection" idea from earlier? One person who shares our DNA is carrying this new life. Spend enough time in close proximity and it spreads to you. That's how every powerful network grows: contact by contact.

---

## Don't fight about the wrapper

One last thing, and it matters.

Christians have a whole catalog of ways to describe what happened here. Some talk about Christ paying the penalty our failures earned. Some say God the Father let us off because Christ delivered the performance we owed. Some use the old sacrificial imagery of being made clean by his blood. Others frame it as a victory: death itself lost the fight.

Every one of those holds up. They're different interfaces pointing at the same underlying system.

If a particular framing does nothing for you, skip it and run with the one that lands. And whatever you do, **don't pick a fight with someone over which interface they prefer.** That's two people screaming about whether to check the same bank balance through the app or the website. Same account. Same money. Stop arguing about the UI.

---

## The bottom line

Here's the realization I want you to walk away with.

Your natural self is the obstinate toy soldier. It will resist the upgrade with total conviction, because it can only see what it's losing. That resistance isn't a sign you're on the wrong path. It's a sign the stakes are real.

But here's the asymmetric bet hiding in plain sight: **you don't have to engineer the transformation yourself.** The founder already went into the product. The proof of concept already shipped. One soldier already came fully alive, and because humanity is one connected network rather than a shelf of isolated toys, that change is already propagating through the whole system.

Your job isn't to climb. Your job is to stop resisting long enough to get close to the one who already made it, and let the new life spread.

The metal thinks it's dying. It's actually being born.

_That's the signal. Everything else is noise._
