Look, I want to talk about something that most people in this space completely misunderstand. Not Bitcoin. Not rates. Not the next macro cycle. I want to talk about **Faith** — and before you close this tab, hear me out, because this is one of the most important mental models you are not running.

Christians use the word "Faith" in two distinct ways, and the difference between them is everything. Let's take them in order.

**The first meaning is simple: belief.** You've reviewed the evidence, you've done your research, and you've concluded that the core claims of Christianity are true. That's it. That's level one. It's basically your investment thesis. You looked at the fundamentals, you stress-tested the model, and you bought in.

Here's the thing — most people get tripped up right here. They ask: _what's virtuous about believing something? Isn't belief just a neutral output of your reasoning process?_ A rational person looks at the evidence and either accepts or rejects a claim. If they get it wrong, they're not a bad person — they're just working with bad data or a flawed model. That's a skill problem, not a character problem.

I used to think this way too. And honestly? I still think it's _partially_ right.

But here's the critical insight I was missing — and that most people are _still_ missing:

**I was assuming the human mind is a perfectly rational machine.** I was assuming that once you've accepted something as true on good evidence, your brain just... holds that position until better evidence comes along.

That is not how humans work. That is not how _you_ work.

## **The Volatility of Human Conviction**

Think about it this way. You know, with high confidence, that your long-term investment thesis is sound. You've done the work. The fundamentals are there. And then the market opens red. And it keeps going red. And your group chat is blowing up with panic. And suddenly, somewhere in your chest, the conviction starts to dissolve — not because new information arrived, but because your _emotions_ launched a coordinated attack on your reasoning.

That's not a logic failure. That's a human failure.

Here's a concrete example. You know — rationally, with full certainty — that a particular business partner has burned you before. The data is clear: unreliable, doesn't deliver, always has an excuse. But they slide into your DMs with a new opportunity, and suddenly you're thinking, _"Maybe this time is different. Maybe I'm being too harsh."_ Your emotions just ran a hostile takeover of your reasoning.

Or think about a first-time founder about to pitch their Series A. They've studied a hundred successful pitches. They know the framework cold. But the moment they walk into that room and the lead partner crosses their arms, the founder's brain — despite all the preparation — starts whispering _"this isn't going to work."_ Not because of new evidence. Because of fear.

**This is the real battle: reason and conviction on one side, emotion and mood on the other.** And the moods don't fight fair.

## **Faith Is Conviction Under Pressure**

Now apply this to Christianity — or frankly, to _any_ deeply held conviction about how the world works.

I'm not asking anyone to accept a belief system their honest reasoning rejects. That's not the point. But let's say your reasoning — after genuine due diligence — tells you the evidence _is_ there. Here's exactly what happens next:

You're going to have a bad week. A deal falls through. Someone close to you gets sick. You're surrounded by smart, skeptical people who think the whole thing is cope. And your emotions will launch a **full blitz** on your conviction. Not because new evidence arrived. Just because the mood changed.

Or — and this one is sneakier — things will be going _great_. You'll see a shortcut. A chance to bend the rules slightly. A moment where it would be really, really _convenient_ if your belief system had a little more flexibility. And your desires will do the same thing: run a blitz on the position you built with your rational mind.

**Faith, in this first sense, is simply the discipline of holding your reasoned conviction through the volatility of your own emotional state.**

It's not blind belief. It's the opposite. It's the commitment to not letting a _mood_ override a _conclusion_. It's the mental equivalent of not panic-selling at the bottom of a drawdown you already modeled and accepted before you entered the position.

And this is why Faith is a _virtue_ — a skill to be developed — and not just a passive intellectual state. Because moods will come for you. That's not a possibility. That's a guarantee. The question is whether you've built the mental infrastructure to hold your position when they do.

## **The Daily Practice Is the Infrastructure**

The first step is simply acknowledging that your conviction will fluctuate — not because the fundamentals changed, but because you're human and your emotional state will change.

The second step is building a _system_ to counteract that drift.

This is why serious practitioners of any discipline — whether it's Christianity, elite athletics, or running a high-performance organization — don't rely on motivation. They rely on **daily process**. Prayer, reading, community, repetition. Not because the truth needs to be re-proven every morning. But because conviction, like any asset, requires active maintenance. It doesn't compound on autopilot. It depreciates through neglect.

If you surveyed a hundred people who walked away from a deeply held belief system, how many of them were actually argued out of it by a superior logical case? My bet: almost none. Most of them just... drifted. The daily inputs stopped. The community dissolved. The mood won by default.

**Don't let the mood win by default.**
